\chapter{Conclusion}

This study presented the design, development, and evaluation of IRIS, a web-based Intellectual Property Rights Information System for the Technology Transfer and Business Development Office of the University of the Philippines Visayas. The system was developed in response to the limitations of the TTBDO's existing practices, which relied on spreadsheets, local file storage, manual monitoring, and fragmented communication. These conditions created difficulties in organizing records, tracking application status, managing deadlines, and coordinating with stakeholders.

The developed system addressed these concerns through a centralized and role-based platform for intellectual property application management. IRIS provided features for secure authentication, role-based access control, guided application initiation, attachment and version management, collaborator handling, status tracking, notifications, dashboards, registry access, public information pages, and controlled interoperability. These features supported the study's goal of improving efficiency, transparency, and organization in the management of intellectual property records and workflows.

The developed system was able to address the identified operational needs of the TTBDO. Its modules reflected the major workflow requirements identified during the study and provided appropriate access and visibility for Technology Generators, UP Officials, TTBDO Admins, and Guests according to their roles. The evaluation results further support the acceptability of the system. End-to-end testing showed that the major workflows operated as intended, performance testing indicated generally good system performance with some public mobile pages requiring further optimization, and penetration testing showed that the system generally enforced its baseline security controls. User Acceptance Testing likewise indicated that the system was acceptable for its intended use.

Overall, the results indicate that the study was able to meet its stated objectives. IRIS demonstrated the potential of a centralized, role-aware, and workflow-oriented platform to improve the handling of intellectual property applications, records, and monitoring activities within the TTBDO. The study also provides a basis for similar initiatives in other academic institutions with related technology transfer and intellectual property management needs.

Based on these findings, several recommendations are proposed for future development and further study. One recommendation is the implementation of a module that integrates with IPOPHL to support more efficient monitoring of application publication and status updates. This may also be extended to include payment tracking and related administrative processes. It is also recommended to develop a content management mechanism that would allow office personnel to update public information and administrative content without modifying the codebase directly.

A dedicated workspace module may also be explored to improve coordination between TTBDO personnel and Technology Generators through more centralized communication and task monitoring features. Future studies may further expand the evaluation of the system by involving a larger pool of users, broader usage conditions, and more extensive security and quality assurance assessments. Finally, future development may consider mobile-focused enhancements to improve accessibility and further optimize performance on mobile devices, including the replacement of heavy client-side chart libraries such as React ApexCharts with lighter alternatives.
