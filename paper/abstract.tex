\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
In the Philippines, Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs) are tasked with facilitating the protection and management of intellectual property (IP) generated in universities. At the University of the Philippines Visayas (UPV), these functions are handled by the Technology Transfer and Business Development Office (TTBDO). At present, the TTBDO uses various third-party software tools and services to record disclosures, store documents, and track IP cases. This setup results in fragmented workflows and potential risks such as incomplete records, inefficient monitoring, and missed deadlines. In response, this study designed, developed, and evaluated IRIS, a web-based Intellectual Rights Information System for the TTBDO. The system streamlines IP portfolio management and supports the application of intellectual property rights by technology generators. Its features include role-based dashboards, an intellectual property application registry, portfolio management and analytics, user management, secure authentication, role-based access control, status tracking, automated notifications, digital submission workflows, and a public portal for published IP applications. IRIS was developed using the Agile software development life cycle, with requirements gathered through interviews and document analysis. The system was evaluated through end-to-end testing, performance testing, penetration testing, and user acceptance testing. Although developed for UPV, the system may be adapted for other academic institutions.
